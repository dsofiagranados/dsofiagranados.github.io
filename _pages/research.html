My research examines how exposure to violence shapes people's long-term political attitudes — especially toward peace, reconciliation, and the reintegration of ex-combatants — and why individuals from the same conflict-affected communities can emerge with such different views. Bridging political science and developmental psychology, I argue that “exposure to violence” is not a single experience: its political legacy depends on when in the life course it occurs and how it is experienced. Much of my empirical work is based in Colombia and Latin America, and throughout I care about studying sensitive, high-risk topics in ways that protect the safety and dignity of participants and field staff.

Dissertation

My dissertation develops a theory of the heterogeneous, long-term effects of violence and asks how they can inform peace-building and early-childhood policy in post-conflict settings. It comprises three projects:

Developmental timing of exposure When

Does the developmental stage at which a person first experiences political violence produce different long-term attitudes toward the reintegration of ex-combatants? Drawing on the concept of sensitive periods in developmental psychology, this project distinguishes early childhood, middle childhood, and adolescence — rather than treating childhood as a single window — using data from the Asking About Violence project and Violence Against Children Surveys.

Armed-group governance and normalization How

Do people who lived under armed-group governance — rule-making, dispute resolution, taxation — hold different political attitudes than those who experienced armed-group presence without governance, or neither? This project theorizes normalization as the mechanism linking sustained exposure to political attitudes and behavior, using subnational variation across Colombian municipalities.

Fostering empathy in conflict-affected children Intervention

Can a Theory of Mind intervention increase empathy toward out-groups among internally displaced and victimized children and adolescents? This project develops and evaluates an intervention aimed at fostering peace-building attitudes and behaviors early in the life course.
